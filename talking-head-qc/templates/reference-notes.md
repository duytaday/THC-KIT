## Template A · Presenter with a proof cutaway

Choose this when a spoken claim can be demonstrated with a document, command, object or result. Open on the viewer's question or a brief shot of the outcome. Let the presenter establish what to look for. Cut to the exact relevant material when it is named. Crop until the critical line is readable at phone size. Hold through the result, keep the speaker audible and return to the presenter for the interpretation. Add a CTA only when the brief calls for one.

QC example: When the presenter says “check the container status,” a legible status row must appear. A terminal window with no visible result fails the proof beat. If the footage is missing, use the presenter for the preview and list the exact pickup shot needed before calling the proof beat complete.

## Template B · Presenter with a short emphasis card

Choose this when one term or transition deserves emphasis. Lift the exact word or short phrase from the approved speech. Use a high-contrast card or darkened frame, then show the presenter, object or action that explains it. Use original typography and test Vietnamese diacritics. Use full captions as required in the brief. In a hardware demo, time the return to the board so the viewer can see the physical action.

QC example: Inspect the card, its first and last readable frames, and the next shot. The card fails if it changes the word, obscures an essential action, or disappears before it can be read. Pick the hold by reading the actual text at normal speed. The R5 sample alone supplies no timing rule.

## Add or refine a reference

Place available clips or screen recordings with audio in references/ as R1.mp4 through R5.mp4. For each, log reference ID, timestamp, observed picture, observed audio, function in the explanation, and a proposed rule. Sample the opening, a proof beat, transitions and ending. Record when sample coverage is partial. Only assign a preferred hold time or cut rate after measuring the clip. If references conflict, use the primary reference named in the brief and state which choice serves the viewer.
